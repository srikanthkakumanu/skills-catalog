# Legal Source Guide

Use this guide when a resource lookup needs careful source routing or licensing judgment. Prefer direct file links only when they are official, public-domain, open-license, or otherwise clearly rights-cleared.

## Books and Ebooks

Prefer:

- Publisher and author pages.
- Project Gutenberg, Standard Ebooks, HathiTrust public-domain records, Internet Archive lending, Open Library, Google Books previews, national library catalogs, WorldCat, and local library systems.
- Retailers and audiobook stores when free access is not lawful.

Check:

- Exact title, author, publication year, edition, ISBN, translator, and territory.
- Public-domain status by jurisdiction when relevant.

Avoid:

- Shadow libraries, unauthorized PDF/EPUB mirrors, forum uploads, and file-hosting links.

## Movies and TV

Prefer:

- Studio, distributor, broadcaster, or platform pages.
- Licensed streaming, rental, purchase, library DVD/Blu-ray, Kanopy, Hoopla, public broadcaster archives, and public-domain film archives.
- IMDb, TMDb, Letterboxd, or official catalogs for disambiguation, not as proof of access rights.

Check:

- Release year, director, country, alternate titles, and region availability.

Avoid:

- Rip sites, screener leaks, torrent indexes, subtitle-bundled mirrors, and unauthorized cyberlockers.

## Music and Audio

Prefer:

- Artist, label, Bandcamp, SoundCloud official profiles, Internet Archive live music collections, Free Music Archive, Jamendo, ccMixter, LibriVox, streaming platforms, stores, and library audio services.

Check:

- Artist, track, album, label, release year, ISRC, and license terms.

Avoid:

- Unauthorized MP3/FLAC download sites, stream-ripping services, leaked albums, and bypass tools.

## Academic Papers and Reports

Prefer:

- DOI landing pages, publisher open-access pages, PubMed Central, arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, SSRN, RePEc, DOAJ, institutional repositories, author pages, OSF, Zenodo, and Google Scholar metadata.

Check:

- DOI, title, authors, journal/conference, year, preprint status, and license.

Response Pattern

For standard lookups, answer with:

1. Resource identification.
2. Direct links first, when  available.
3. Other access options grouped by access type.
4. Notes on region, edition, or uncertainty.
5. A concise refusal plus alternatives when the requested access path is unauthorized.
